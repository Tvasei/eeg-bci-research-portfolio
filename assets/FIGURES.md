# Figure sources

The figures were cropped from the research papers and rendered as PNGs. Axis scales, units, legends and data marks follow the source figures. The portfolio cover is a title card.

## Wearable motor-imagery BCI

Tahereh Vasei, Mohammad Ali Saber, Alireza Nahvy and Zainalabedin Navabi. *An Efficient RTL Design for a Wearable Brain–Computer Interface*. IET Computers & Digital Techniques, 2024, article 5596468. © 2024 the authors. The published figures are reproduced under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Page margins, captions and surrounding text were removed. [Paper and original figures](https://doi.org/10.1049/2024/5596468)

- [Figure 17, p. 12](figures/bci-architecture.png) shows the signal path in two hardware designs, including the shared filter and spatial-pattern, variance and classifier stages.
- [Figure 6, p. 5](figures/bci-csp.png) shows the two motor-imagery classes plotted in raw channel space and after spatial filtering.
- [Figure 12, p. 9](figures/bci-filter-bank.png) shows the third filter-bank design, which shares arithmetic across channels and uses RAM for filter state.

## Paired dry and gel EEG

Tahereh Vasei, Maryam Ravan and Gary Hasey. *ACCA: Asymmetric ComBat–CORAL alignment for gel-to-dry EEG transfer in suicidal-ideation classification*. Author manuscript. Figures are credited to the manuscript authors.

- [Figure 2, p. 9](figures/dry-gel-spectra.png) shows median eyes-closed power spectra for both headsets. Shading spans the interquartile range across participants.
- [Figure 4, p. 11](figures/dry-gel-transfer.png) shows changes in clinical AUC relative to raw transfer. Lines show paired participant-bootstrap 95% intervals.

## Multi-cohort CVAE–GATv2

Tahereh Vasei, Maryam Ravan and Gary Hasey. *Reference-conditioned CVAE harmonization with graph attention for multi-cohort resting-state EEG classification of major depressive disorder*. Revised author manuscript. Figures are credited to the manuscript authors.

- [Figure 2, p. 42](figures/cvae-umap.png) compares UMAP projections of EEG before and after harmonization and of the classifier embeddings. The rows color the observations by source cohort or diagnosis.
- [Figure 3, p. 47](figures/cvae-occlusion.png) shows the confidence change after masking each electrode, together with subject-bootstrap intervals. The [scalp map](figures/cvae-scalp.png) is panel (a) of the same figure and appears in the cohort study section.

## Brain responses to electroacupuncture (TEAS)

Tahereh Vasei, Harshil Gediya, Maryam Ravan, Anand Santhanakrishnan, David Mayor and Tony Steffert. *Investigating Brain Responses to Transcutaneous Electroacupuncture Stimulation: A Deep Learning Approach*. Algorithms, 2024, article 477. © 2024 the authors. The published figure is reproduced under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [Paper and original figure](https://doi.org/10.3390/a17110477)

- [Figure 5, p. 15](figures/teas-saliency.png) shows topographic distributions of aggregated saliency maps for the four TEAS frequencies (2.5, 10, 80, and sham 160 pps). Warmer colors indicate higher electrode importance.

The licenses of the published IET and Algorithms papers apply to their figures. The two author manuscripts retain their own rights.
